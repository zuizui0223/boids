## Scientific objective

The original 2024 `Molossus nigricans` (Krivoruchko et al., PNAS DOI `10.1073/pnas.2321724121`) analysis used 10 bats and 4299 observed 5-s windows; acoustic crowding >75 calls/5s coincided with low attack success even within six bats and within coarse flight-time thirds. Original authors already reported that directional crowding result. The next genuinely distinct source must have **separately measured acoustic exposure AND locomotor/foraging or receiver performance** to test what high interference does beyond existing descriptive outcomes.

Two *independent* candidate studies with source-backed scientific use cases:

1. **Goldshtein et al. 2025 PNAS**, DOI `10.1073/pnas.2407810122`, PMCID `PMC12002023`, original 2025 Mendeley dataset DOI `10.17632/k7pt2j84f7.1`, CC BY 4.0, published `Code.zip` (~2.58 MB) and `Data.zip` (~83.7 MB). Source measured simultaneous `Rhinopoma microphyllum` on-bat acoustics and group flight; original paper ALREADY found severe acoustic masking and rapid physical spreading. Not an independently measured prey-capture task. Public Mendeley API returned 401/404 in earlier source admission; do not bypass it.
2. **Gilmour et al. 2021 JEB**, DOI `10.1242/jeb.242715`, PMCID `PMC8601711`, Mendeley DOI `10.17632/gb45nfkkk5.1`, CC BY 4.0. Source used alternating 5-minute silent vs ultrasound deterrent blocks at 3 sites ×3 nights. Original paper already showed 30% reduction in bat activity, altered flight speed/tortuosity and `Pipistrellus pygmaeus` feeding buzz rate/bandwidth. Its **1167 independently tracked flight trajectories are NOT 1167 established unique biological bat individuals**; individual reuse/trajectory identity cannot be assumed. Only 9 independent nights and 3 sites; site×night is the relevant experimental block.
