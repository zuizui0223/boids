## What is and is not an ecological result

**Supported at this narrow observational level:** in one genuine 2020 Zeeland project, two actually tagged gull species differ in how often original GPS-used points fall on high-classifier-score GIC tidal-wetland pixels, even after equalizing the original phase×sun four source cells and comparable May–July calendar months. This is a spatial use *association* conditional on these birds and source, not an observed used-available preference, tidalflat area, or adaptive ecological niche mechanism.

**Weak/exploratory:** species may differ in DAY vs NIGHT use of the same native RF class, but its bird-bootstrap contrast was post hoc, not multiplicity corrected, and one tide-phase ghost produced nearly the same contrast. Species-level local daylight schedules/tag duty cycles, colony locations and age/sex could explain some differences.

**Not established:** rising vs falling tide effect at same observed NAP stage or species-specific tide×solar interaction on native GPS wetland-class use; previous v111 source-supported GPS movement tide×sun interaction also not established. No evidence that either species adjusts feeding/roosting to a truly exposed mudflat accessible time window or has a physiological circatidal clock.

**CRITICAL: GIC score 50 is native random-forest agreement in a static 2017–2019 map that includes flats, marshes and mangroves, and GPS fix point errors are comparable to 30m pixels.** It is NOT calibrated biological site suitability, count of occupied hectares, local 2020 instantaneous dry mudflat state, prey, real foraging or activity fraction. Both Roompotsluis stations reflect different water bodies separated by the Oosterschelde barrier; a 20km circle does not ensure all GPS local tides share the gauge clock. Sampled two species at one colony area have common nonrandom observation schedules. These data alone do not establish a global species-specific temporal niche law.
