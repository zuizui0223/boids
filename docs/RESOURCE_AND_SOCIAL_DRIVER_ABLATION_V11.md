# v11 — What makes the simulated niche: habitat targets or Boids forces?

**Study status:** completed known-truth **synthetic** ablation, 2026-10-27. No new wild animals, observation records, species-specific movement coefficients, competition data, or empirical niche forecasts were used. v4–v10 results are preserved; neither the unsuccessful primary bat v6 nor pigeon v8 contrast is reopened.

## Research question

The v10 experiment propagated a finite set of separation/cohesion coefficients into 3D utilization distributions and obtained a stable predicted species partition axis under altered resource configurations. But each *pseudo-species* was already given a distinct environmental target. Was the resulting species niche information generated by social Boids rules, or mostly **imposed by targets**? Did within-species individual identity similarly depend on pre-installed personal targets?

The v11 design was deliberately made more discriminating: matched stochastic forcing and initial positions under four habitat-target configurations and social Boids on/off. It is an in-simulator **intervention** and an explanatory negative control, not a field causal test.

## Fixed model and generative equation

For 12 synthetic agents (two arbitrary labels of six; each label contains three intraspecific dyads), positions and velocities follow an Euler-integrated stochastic paired motion model:

\[
\dot{x}_i=v_i,\qquad
\dot v_i =-K\,(x_i-T_i)-\lambda v_i+O(x_i-T_i)+\varepsilon_i(t)
+1_{\mathrm{social}}\left(\gamma_\alpha -\frac{\alpha}{d_{i,j}^3}\right)(x_j-x_i),
\]

where \(T_i\) is an **assigned coordinate target**, not a measured food patch, \(O\) is a stipulated weak planar rotation term and \(j\) is the sole within-dyad neighbour. The real-valued dynamics are not physically constrained bat/pigeon aerodynamics. They do not model interspecific social reactions, competition, actual depletion, learning, or independent habitat selection inference.

The v9/v10 source-equivalent force line is retained: \(d_0=10\,\mathrm m\), \(\kappa_0=-0.003\,\mathrm{s}^{-2}\), \(\gamma_\alpha=\kappa_0+\alpha/d_0^3\) for \(\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}\). Initial distance is always **25 m**, intentionally outside that source geometry. All agents in both pseudo-species have **identical initial pair geometries** before resource-target differences are applied. Source-equivalent coefficient values are illustrative, not estimated from v8 GPS.

Time step 0.1 model seconds, 650 steps, 200-step burn-in, every fourth subsequent point retained. Twelve fully specified independent random-number generator seeds 20261028–20261039, common random numbers across on/off social force and across candidate alphas. Dynamics use preset planar and vertical harmonic target terms, Gaussian acceleration disturbances and velocity drag. Three force parameters are a *finite illustrative candidate set*, neither a confidence region nor a complete identified set.

Scenarios, defined by the two assigned pseudo-species' opposed offsets from the same center:

| Setting | Species center X offset | Species center Z offset |
|---|---:|---:|
| Same targets | 0 m | 0 m |
| Vertical contrast | ±0 m | ±8 m |
| Horizontal contrast | ±9 m | ±0 m |
| Both contrasts | ±9 m | ±8 m |

Within each species, both dyad roles have assigned vertical offsets of ±2.5 m; the **individual-target ablation** sets these to zero, holding everything else fixed.

For the two species occupancy arrays, compute \(I(S;XY)\) and \(I(S;Z\mid XY)=I(S;XYZ)-I(S;XY)\), and for the six individuals per species compute their pooled \(I(i;Z\mid XY,S)\). Coarse 4×4×4 and finer 7×7×7 fixed categorical grids are evaluated without post-hoc cell adjustment; all samples are treated as finite occupancy frequencies, **not** independent animal observations. For candidate models, also report joint \(P(S,XYZ)\) total-variation distance.

## 1. Species information comes primarily from explicitly assigned environmental differences

Coarse grid, **alpha=1.0**; values are nats. With the same habitat targets, pseudo-species labels carry almost no information about the realized horizontal or conditional-height state.

| Environmental contrast | Boids social on I(S;XY) | Boids social on I(S;Z|XY) | Social off I(S;XY) | Social off I(S;Z|XY) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| None | 0.00084 | 0.00214 | 0.00117 | 0.00113 |
| Vertical | 0.00084 | **0.61021** | 0.00117 | **0.64256** |
| Horizontal | **0.44384** | 0.00352 | **0.54931** | 0.00488 |
| Both | **0.44384** | **0.19174** | **0.54931** | **0.11747** |

With Boids social **off**, the environmental contrast still generates strong, correctly oriented species information. Therefore **v10's axis switching cannot be attributed to spontaneous emergence from Boids alone**. The initial state was species-exchangeable: this effect is not merely species-specific start coordinates, but it is **directly forced by our species-specific target coordinates**.

## 2. Social Boids can redistribute the strength of environmental partitioning

For the *both contrasts* world (alpha=1, coarse grain), switching social force from off to on reduces horizontal species information **by 0.10547 nats** and increases conditional-vertical species information **by 0.07427 nats**. A paired stochastic-seed bootstrap (1,200 draws, twelve seeds, shared on/off noise) has percentile ranges:

- I(S;XY) social on − off: **[-0.1233,-0.0869]** nats.
- I(S;Z|XY) social on − off: **[+0.0494,+0.1013]** nats.

The finer grain retains signs but reduces magnitude to **−0.0274** and **+0.0242** nats, respectively (boot intervals [-0.0393,-0.0173], [+0.0123,+0.0387]). **The numerical effect is grain-dependent.** This is a model-intervention effect under one fixed synthetic architecture; it cannot be read as ecological evidence of cross-species competition.

As a caution, with vertical-only resource contrast the coarse-grid Boids force instead *decreases* the conditional-vertical species information (0.64256 → 0.61021). Hence even in this toy model, the direction of a social-force effect depends on which environmental dimensions differ.

## 3. Individual vertical identity is also mostly stipulated

Within a single shared species-level target environment, with social Boids **on**, I(individual;Z|XY,species) averaged **0.27212 nats** with ±2.5 m role targets but only **0.03529 nats** when the per-individual z targets were removed. With social Boids off the corresponding numbers were **0.23509 → 0.02193**. This strongly limits the v10 interpretive reach: **reproducing repeated individual profiles by prescribing personal response goals does not explain how wild individuals acquired or maintained those goals.**

## 4. Whole-distribution prediction versus coarse ecological summaries

With the same synthetic generator and *known* newly imposed target coordinates, a Monte-Carlo forward prediction from the first eight seeds is scored on the last four independent stochastic seeds. The baseline deliberately assumes both species share the original undifferentiated resource center. Holding everything else fixed, the known-environment simulator (true alpha=1; social force on) has positive relative log-score gains on coarse-grid heldout frequencies:

| New target environment | Gain over resource-blind original-world forecast (nats/fix) |
|---|---:|
| Vertical contrast | +1.13058 |
| Horizontal contrast | +0.45604 |
| Both contrasts | +1.45729 |

Across the three candidate alphas, coarse-grid gain ranges were [1.11494,1.13059], [0.45604,0.46130], [1.45066,1.46791]. These are **deliberate known-environment forward-simulator comparisons, not data-driven prediction of unknown resource fields**. Their success means including the *correct, supplied toy environment* is needed to predict these synthetic outcomes; it is not evidence that the true environmental preference can be deduced from animal trajectories.

A coarse niche-axis sign can be stable while fine occupancy maps differ. For the two-target-contrast world, the maximum pairwise total variation between candidate alpha's joint \(P(S,X,Y,Z)\) is **0.0555** at coarse grain and **0.0740** at fine grain. Thus accepting a common niche-axis prediction is weaker than accepting a unique pointwise utilization map. Quantitative ecological forecasts must propagate both force uncertainty and environmental-response uncertainty, not just report a single fitted policy.

## Biological relevance and explicit limitations

- **batter:** the synthetic model supports the *logical possibility* of individual vertical-profile persistence without spatial exclusion; it does **not** explain why individual policies are stable in bats.
- **ODSP:** a conditional niche information value and a held-out forecast are different estimands. The toy holdout is a new random seed from **the same** simulator, not cross-species transfer.
- **louis:** preserving an experienced environmental state requires actual measured state and availability fields; the toy coordinate target is not such a field.
- **mina:** breeding patch concentration is a demographic/patch-availability process absent here; short-time dyadic Boids cannot by itself explain penguin colony contraction.

**Not established:** self-organized origin of resource preferences, actual interspecific niche partitioning, a competition-induced niche shift, learned personal movement memory, wild-bat/pigeon steering coefficients, or multiday real 3D niche forecasts. Previously frozen observed bat v6 and pigeon v8 primary partner-versus-control contrasts remain non-supportive and unchanged.

## Next falsifiable empirical bridge

Fit independently calibrated observation-error-aware movement kernels against long-duration multianimal 3D trajectories, with habitat/feeding targets measured independently of the tracks. In new sites/seasons, predict **which partition axis and within-species profile information persist** while holding out species/individuals, and compare against *resource-only*, *inertia-only*, *social-only*, and *combined* models. Test the independent **environmental target measurement** before allowing the combined model to claim out-of-context ecological prediction. This avoids simply coding the desired niche pattern into the simulator.
